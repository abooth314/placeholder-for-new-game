Graphics & performance

World geometry

A smaller radius creates stronger negative curvature. Applying rebuilds the current battlefield and its navigation graph.

Sounds of the battlefield

Steel, bows, hoofbeats, siege, magic, and healing are synthesized locally. Distance and stereo placement use the camera's hyperbolic frame. A bounded voice bank keeps large battles under control.

A real H³ world

Positions live on the Lorentz hyperboloid. Hills are normal displacements of an H² ground surface. Navigation uses sampled surface lengths; model vertices use exponential maps. The atlas is a chart of this world.

Read the battle
Infantry holds the front. Spearmen screen cavalry. Archers and mages stay behind friendly troops. Knights seek a flank. Clerics follow and heal. Catapults bombard from a distance.
Squads gather before advancing, share objectives, and fall back when their local balance of strength turns against them. Friendly presence matters. Change their relationships at the table of kingdoms.
The world has solid 3D models and ordinary simulation time. Press V for free flight: move above or between the models, and look around with right-drag. Distances, movement, camera frames, and projectile paths use that geometry. The 2D atlas compresses distant land.
Fortresses are rebuilt after defeat so the war can continue. Open War council to deploy troops, change limits, or turn off combat and explore.
Implementation notes: terrain navigation is a finite sampled graph, contact is lightweight intrinsic separation, projectile arcs use a chosen game-gravity profile, and shadows are inexpensive ground decals. The battlefield is finite; the simulation can run continuously. No external assets, libraries, or network access are required.
